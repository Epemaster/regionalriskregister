03 / Assessment design

Risk intelligence you can trace.

Identify hazards & dependencies

Define geography, climate and ecological drivers, potentially affected people, assets and ecosystems.

Assess exposure, vulnerability & impact

Document severity by relevant scenario and time horizon with evidence and scientific confidence.

Evaluate preparedness & ownership

Distinguish policy readiness, operating controls, legal responsibilities and funding responsibilities.

Analyse cascades & financial effects

Identify correlated shocks and transmission to insurers, lenders, service provision, businesses and public budgets.

Verify interventions & residual risk

Record baseline, counterfactual, monitored results, data quality, review date and residual exposure.

Methodological foundations

ISO 31000 provides a general risk-management framing. CERAC BCRA and EUCRA, informed by ISO 14091 and the IPCC, offer a climate-and-ecosystem-specific assessment reference.

Three separate dimensions underpin BCRA urgency:

  • Impact severity: limited, substantial, critical, catastrophic
  • Scientific confidence: low, medium, high
  • Policy readiness: low, medium, advanced, very advanced

The published urgency lookup has five action categories. It is not a mathematical multiplication of numeric scores. Belgian thresholds are not directly transferable to a Dutch region.

RRR additions: inherent/current/residual states, evidence registers, risk owner/implementation owner, scenario dependency graphs and financeability tests.

CERAC methodological report (2025), sections 3–4 and 6: scenario timing, uncertainty, normative priorities, cross-system effects and gaps warrant explicit documentation. The initial BCRA did not fully quantify cascading impacts.

05 / Decisions & capital

From risk intelligence to resilience investment.

Regional cost & loss

Assess physical loss, ecosystem-service disruption, service interruption and distributional exposure, including uncertainty and double-counting controls.

Financing & responsibility

Separate public responsibility, private beneficiaries, insurance coverage, investment sponsors and eligible contracting parties.

Measured performance

Design verifiable baselines and counterfactuals for potential resilience-performance agreements, subject to legal, financial and scientific validation.

Avoided loss is a potential economic benefit, not automatically a bankable revenue stream. Financing requires an identifiable payer, enforceable contractual basis, verified outcome and appropriate allocation of risk.
06 / Cross-cutting systemic layer

Planetary signals. Regional consequences.

Compound & cascading stress

Explore plausible, evidence-linked combinations: low river discharge and industrial disruption; drought, heat and food systems; or flooding, insurance exposure and local fiscal strain. Test correlated dependencies without double counting losses.

AMOC exploratory scenario

AMOC weakening is retained as a separate RRR stress-test research topic. Specific regional precipitation, temperature, ecosystem and economic pathways must be sourced, modelled and uncertainty-tested before assigning any regional likelihood or magnitude.

Use and validation: This is an independently developed methodological prototype, not an official CERAC, EU or Dutch public authority assessment. Belgian urgency classifications refer only to the original national assessment. The Rivierenland topics do not contain validated regional scores, risk probabilities, expected damages or investment recommendations. Data licences, independent quality control and affected-stakeholder review are required before regional publication.
